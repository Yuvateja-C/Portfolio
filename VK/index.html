01 — About

Systems thinking,
with a bias for shipping.

Varshitha is a Computer Science undergraduate specializing in AI & Data Science, and the founder of Glyphora, an AI-powered digital solutions venture. Her technical foundation — Python, SQL, machine learning, and data analysis — grew out of building things, not just studying them.

What pulled her toward product management wasn't a course, it was a pattern: the best solutions she built only mattered once she understood the person stuck on the other side of the problem. That's the lens she now brings to product work as an AI Product Manager — pairing systems thinking with the kind of user empathy that shows up in projects like What's My Move, built for students her own resume history knows well: the ones without a campus placement cushion.

Day to day, that means defining problems precisely, running structured, hypothesis-driven experiments, and translating technical complexity into decisions non-technical stakeholders can actually act on — a habit formed early, building executive dashboards from raw behavioral data as a Data Analyst at the DST-CURIE-AI Centre.

OpsMind AI

An industrial decision-intelligence platform that turns fragmented maintenance and compliance records into explainable, ranked recommendations — instead of one more place to search documents.

Personal product · Live on GitHub

What's My Move

A 12-question career-direction quiz for CS students who don't have a placement yet — it scores them against real tech paths, then writes them a short, specific, LLM-generated pep talk.

Problem

Final-year students at colleges without strong placement support don't lack ambition — they lack a next step that actually fits their profile.

Approach

Branch, current status, work style, tools known, personality, and a "2-year dream" feed a scoring model; the chosen path gets a message generated live via the Claude API, tuned to encourage without sugar-coating.

Lesson learned

Originally prototyped as a Claude Artifact. Porting it to a standalone deploy means swapping sandboxed storage and API calls for a real serverless proxy — the exact gap between a prototype and a shippable product.

04 — Product Thinking

Two teardowns.

Written to show how she reads a product, not just uses one. These are original analysis pieces, not case studies of prior published work.

01

Product Teardown

GitHub Copilot — from autocomplete to orchestration layer

Copilot launched in 2021 doing one thing: finishing your line of code. By 2026 it plans multi-file changes, runs its own terminal commands, opens pull requests while you sleep via an async cloud agent, and — with Agent HQ — orchestrates third-party agents like Claude and Codex from a single control plane. The product didn't get a feature, it changed categories: from a writing assistant to the interface for deciding which agent does the work.

The interesting PM bet is the pricing shift to usage-based "AI Credits" landing right as agent workflows get more compute-hungry, not less. That's a real tension: the feature GitHub is betting the product's future on (autonomous, multi-step agents) is also the one most likely to make a user's monthly bill unpredictable. Holding both — reducing adoption friction on the thing you need people to trust, while still metering its heaviest use — is the harder problem than shipping the agent itself.

02

Product Teardown

Way2News — winning distribution, defending trust

Way2News built its edge on being hyperlocal in a media market built for metros — short, vernacular stories in eight Indian languages, reaching readers in districts and villages that national apps write past. It's real distribution: tens of millions of downloads and Series B funding from WestBridge Capital on the strength of that positioning alone.

The harder question sits underneath the growth story. Short-form, vernacular news is exactly the format where speed and trust pull against each other — the pressure to publish fast in more languages is also pressure on verification. The company's own messaging leans hard on "trustworthy" as a differentiator, which tells you where it thinks the real risk is. The PM question worth asking isn't "how do we scale hyperlocal further," it's "what does the trust layer look like once you're publishing at a pace no editorial team can fully review."

06 — How She Works

One process, applied every time.

01

Research

Behavioral data and direct signal first — the DST-CURIE dashboards started here, not with a solution in mind.

02

Ideation

Widen before narrowing — Kairo's "future self" concept came from reframing habit-tracking as an emotional problem, not a data one.

03

Validation

Cheap tests before expensive builds — Kairo's concept was checked with real users before a line of product code was written.

04

Prioritization

RICE and MoSCoW to decide what earns a build slot — and what explicitly doesn't, yet.

05

Prototype

Real data models over mockups — OpsMind's six schema migrations exist because a fake dashboard hides real problems.

06

Testing

Put it in front of the exact person it's for — What's My Move was shaped by what actually worried final-year students, not assumptions about them.

07

Launch

Ship the smallest version that's honestly useful — then say plainly what it doesn't do yet.

08

Iteration

Treat the gap between prototype and product as the actual work — not a footnote after launch.
